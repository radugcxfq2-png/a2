Guide I

The Spice Storage Guide

Spices do not go off. They go quiet. How and where you keep them decides whether that takes four years or four months.

Everything that makes a spice worth using is in its volatile oils, and those oils have four enemies: heat, light, air and moisture. Most kitchens store spices in the one place that supplies all four, which is a rack on the wall beside the cooker.

Moving the jars two metres, into a cupboard or drawer, is the single most useful thing you can do for your cooking this week.

1

Choose the right place

The ideal home is cool, dark and dry: a drawer, a cupboard away from the oven, or a shelf inside the pantry. Avoid the cupboard directly above the hob or kettle, where steam rises every day, and any spot that catches the afternoon sun.

A drawer with the jars lying flat, labels up, is the best arrangement of all. You see everything at a glance and nothing hides at the back.

2

Use airtight jars, and small ones

Glass jars with tight lids are ideal. Tins are also good and keep out light. Paper packets and bags with a folded top let in air and should be decanted. Choose jars that match how much you use: a half-empty jar is half full of air, and air is what dulls the spice.

  • Label each jar with the name and the month you opened it.
  • Never shake a jar over a steaming pan. Steam enters, the spice clumps and fades. Tip some into your hand or a spoon instead.
  • Use a dry spoon every time.
3

Buy less, more often

A large bag of ground cumin is no bargain if half of it is tasteless by the time you reach it. Buy ground spices in the smallest size sold and replace them yearly. Buy whole spices in slightly larger amounts, since they hold their flavour for several years.

4

Know how long each kind lasts

SpiceFormBest within
Peppercorns, cumin, coriander, clovesWhole3–4 years
Cinnamon sticks, nutmeg, star aniseWhole3–4 years
Turmeric, paprika, ginger, cinnamonGround1–2 years
Chilli powder, cayenneGround1 year
Oregano, thyme, mint, bayDried leaf1–2 years
Any blendMixed, ground6 months

Red spices such as paprika and chilli fade fastest, losing colour as well as flavour. Some cooks keep them in the fridge door in a tightly closed jar, which helps as long as the jar is never left open in a steamy kitchen.

5

Test before you trust

Dates on jars are a guide. Your nose is the test. Tip a little into your palm, rub it with a thumb and smell. A good spice announces itself. A tired one smells faintly of dust. If it is only a little faded, use half as much again. If there is nothing there, let it go. The Freshness Checker gives a quick estimate from the age and storage.

6

Clear out the rack once a year

Pick a day, take every jar out and line them up. Smell each one. Put back only what passes. Write a short list of what needs replacing and, just as usefully, a list of what you never used and should not buy again. Most households cook with about a dozen spices. A rack of twelve fresh jars will serve you far better than one of forty forgotten ones.
