The Spice Blends Guide

A spice blend is a recipe with only one step. Understand how the parts fit together and you can make any classic mix, then bend it to your own taste.

Shop-bought blends are convenient, but they are ground months before you open them and usually padded with salt. A blend made at home takes five minutes, costs less and tastes of far more.

You need a set of measuring spoons, a bowl and a jar. A grinder helps but is not essential.

1

How a blend is built

Nearly every blend in the world has the same three layers.

  • A base, making up about half. Mild, earthy spices that carry the others: coriander, cumin, paprika.
  • A character, about a third. The spices that make the blend recognisable: cinnamon and cardamom in garam masala, chilli in taco seasoning.
  • An accent, a small pinch. Strong spices used with restraint: cloves, nutmeg, cayenne, black pepper.

When a home-made blend tastes harsh, the accent is too heavy. When it tastes flat, the character is too light.

2

Six classic blends

BlendPartsUse it for

Ras el hanout2 each cumin, coriander, ginger, 1 each cinnamon, turmeric, paprika, pepperTagines, couscous, roast carrots
Mild curry powder4 coriander, 2 cumin, 2 turmeric, 1 ginger, 1 mustard, a pinch of chilliEveryday curries, soups, rice
Italian herb mix3 oregano, 3 basil, 2 thyme, 2 marjoram, 1 rosemaryTomato sauces, roast potatoes
Baking spice4 cinnamon, 2 ginger, 1 nutmeg, 1 allspice, ½ clovesCakes, porridge, stewed fruit

A “part” is any measure you like, as long as you use the same one throughout. The Spice Blend Mixer turns these ratios into teaspoons for any batch size.

3

Toast whole, then grind

If you have whole seeds, toast them in a dry pan for a minute or two before grinding. Toast each spice separately or add them in order of size, large seeds first, because small seeds burn while big ones are still warming. Let them cool fully before grinding, or the steam will make the powder clump.

4

Leave the salt out

Keep salt out of the jar and add it to the dish instead. A salt-free blend lets you season generously without the food becoming too salty, and it means the same blend works in a marinade, a soup and a dry rub.

5

Adjust it to your taste

Make a small batch first. Stir a pinch into a spoonful of plain yogurt or warm oil and taste it, since raw powder on the tongue tells you little. Then change one thing at a time.

  • Too sharp or bitter? Add more of the base.
  • Too dull? Add more of the character spice.
  • Missing warmth? A little cinnamon or allspice.
  • Missing heat? Cayenne, a quarter teaspoon at a time.
6

Store and use

Keep blends in small airtight jars, labelled with the date, in a dark cupboard. Ground blends are at their best for about three months and still good at six. Make only what you will use in that time.

As a starting point, use two teaspoons of blend for a dish that serves four, added after the onions and cooked for under a minute before any liquid goes in.
